# Writing a story script for ClipForge

ClipForge turns each short chunk of your script into one **scene**: a place (background), 1–3 things on screen (characters, animals, objects), a camera move, the voice-over and captions. The clearer each chunk says *where we are* and *what we see*, the better the pictures match.

## How ClipForge reads your script

| What you write | What happens |
|---|---|
| A blank line | Starts a new paragraph. Scenes never cross paragraphs. |
| 1–2 sentences (up to ~220 characters) | One scene, about 8–15 seconds of voice-over. Longer paragraphs are split automatically. |
| A place word (forest, village, river bank, temple, city street…) | That scene's background. If you don't name one, the last place stays. |
| Concrete nouns (girl, elephant, firefly, lantern, boat…) | Things on screen. They're looked up in the object database first, then drawn in your art style if missing. |
| A time word (dawn, sunset, night) | The background is lit to match. |
| A number (40%, 3 crore, 1,200 years) | A big animated stat card. |
| “A quote in quotation marks” | A quote card. |
| More than 60 scenes | Only the first 60 are used. |

## The structure (copy this)

Each line below is **one paragraph**. Leave a blank line between them.

```
1. HOOK: one surprising line. Name the place and one striking thing.
2. WORLD: where and when. A place + time + 1–2 things we see there.
3. CHARACTER: who it's about. Give them a simple, visual description (an old fisherman, a girl with a lantern).
4. WANT: what they want or what is normal. Same character, same name.
5. CHANGE: something happens. A new thing appears (a storm, a tiger, a letter).
6. STRUGGLE: what they do about it. Action verbs: walks, climbs, follows, builds.
7. TURN: the key moment. Use a number or a quote here if you have one.
8. RESULT: what changed. Same place at a new time (sunset, dawn) works well.
9. CLOSE: the one line you want people to remember (+ a call to action if needed).
```

For a 60-second short use 6–8 paragraphs of one sentence each. For a 2–3 minute story use 9–12 paragraphs of two sentences each.

## Rules that give the best pictures

1. **One place per paragraph.** Say it plainly: *“In a quiet forest at night, …”*. A new place means a new paragraph.
2. **Name what we see, 1–3 things.** *“…fireflies drift between the trees while an owl watches.”* → firefly, tree, owl.
3. **Use everyday names.** “elephant”, not “pachyderm”. “firefly”, not “luminous beetle”. Plurals are fine.
4. **Keep the same name for a character all the way through.** If she is “the girl with the lantern” in scene 3, don't call her “the young traveller” in scene 6. That way the same picture is reused.
5. **Describe the character once, early.** Age, role or clothing (an old farmer in a turban, a schoolgirl in a blue uniform).
6. **Feelings need a visible thing.** Not “hope filled the air”, but “a single lamp glowed in the window”. Wind, light, love and sound can't be drawn, so they're skipped.
7. **Put numbers in sentences of their own** so the stat card isn't crowded: *“Each night, 2,000 fireflies light up this valley.”*
8. **Short sentences read better aloud.** Aim for 8–20 words.
9. **Avoid lists of many objects.** More than three things in one sentence and only the first three appear.
10. **Write the time of day** when it matters: dawn, morning, sunset, night.

## Example: good vs weak

**Weak** (abstract, no place, nothing to draw):

> The beautiful lights made everyone feel magical and happy that evening.

**Good** (place, time, three visible things):

> At sunset in the old forest, fireflies rose from the grass. A small girl held up her lantern, and an owl watched from a branch.

## Full sample (shadow puppet or ink wash work well)

```
Every summer night, a tiny village in Kerala glows with fireflies.

The village sits beside a quiet river, under tall coconut trees. At dusk, the fishermen pull their boats onto the sand.

Meera is a girl with a lantern who loves the fireflies more than anything.

Each night she walks to the river bank and counts them, one by one.

One evening, the river bank is dark. Not a single firefly rises from the grass.

Meera asks the old fisherman why. He points at the new bright lamps on the bridge.

“Fireflies need the dark to find each other,” he says.

So Meera and the villagers switch off the bridge lamps after sunset.

The next night, more than 1,000 fireflies return to the river bank.

Sometimes, to see the light, we just have to turn ours off.
```

## Hindi voice-over (हिन्दी)

- On the Script page set **Voice-over → हिन्दी Hindi**, then pick a voice: Alpha or Beta (female), Omega or Psi (male). These are Kokoro voices that run on this computer. The Windows Hindi voice (Kalpana or Hemant) also appears once you add it in Settings → Time & language → Speech.
- Write in **Devanagari**. End sentences with **।** (or . ? !). Each paragraph is still one scene.
- Write numbers as digits (१,००० or 1,000) so the stat card can show them.
- Captions show your exact words, and Whisper only provides the timing. On-screen titles are written in Hindi too.
- Names for the pictures still work in Hindi scripts (मीरा → Meera's cast character), because the planner names people and places in plain English behind the scenes.

## Quick checklist before you press Create

- [ ] Blank line between every paragraph
- [ ] Every paragraph names a place (or clearly stays in the last one)
- [ ] Every paragraph has 1–3 things we can see
- [ ] Characters keep one name throughout
- [ ] No paragraph longer than two sentences
- [ ] Numbers and quotes have their own sentence
- [ ] The first line is a hook; the last line is the takeaway
