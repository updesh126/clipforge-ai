# Script tags

Put **[tags]** in your script to tell ClipForge what a scene shows and which motion graphic, camera move, transition or atmosphere it uses. Tags are never spoken and never appear in captions. Anything you don't tag is chosen automatically, so you only tag what you care about.

New to scripts? Start with [Writing a script](#/docs/script-guide), then come back here for the full list.

```text
[title: How UPI Changed India] [place: busy market street in Mumbai] Ten years ago, almost every payment here was made in cash.

Today a vegetable seller simply holds up a QR code. [scene: a vegetable seller on a wooden cart holding up a phone next to a QR code sticker]

Over 3.5 billion payments now happen every month. [bigStat value=3.5 suffix=" billion" label="payments every month"]

The idea travelled far beyond India, and now shops in France accept it too. [place: small shop in Paris] [mapInvestigation country=France]

By evening, millions of small payments have moved quietly across the country. [time: sunset] [pullOut]
```

## How to write a tag

| Form | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `[name]` | `[rainFx]` | Use this effect in this scene |
| `[name key=value …]` | `[bigStat value=900 suffix=" million"]` | …with these settings (quote values that have spaces) |
| `[name value value …]` | `[creatorCounter 200 "real leads"]` | Values only: they fill the settings in the order listed under **Values in order** (here `value`, then `text`). Mix in `key=value` for anything else. |
| `[kind: name]` | `[transition: irisWipe]` | Same, saying what it is (optional — the name already tells ClipForge) |

## Scene direction tags — what the picture shows

These are the most useful tags of all: they make the AI painting (or the vector drawing) match your words.

| Tag | Example | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `[place: setting]` | `[place: old market street in Mumbai]` | Where the scene happens. **Carries on** to the next scenes until you write another `[place: …]` — or until the story clearly moves somewhere else (a server room, another country). Also `[location: …]`, `[setting: …]`. |
| `[scene: picture]` | `[scene: a seller holding up a phone next to a QR code]` | Exactly what this scene's picture shows, in one concrete sentence. Beats the planner's guess. Also `[show: …]`, `[shot: …]`. |
| `[time: …]` | `[time: night]` | `day`, `sunset`, `night` or `dawn` (also morning, evening, dusk, sunrise). Carries on like `[place: …]`. |
| `[title: text]` | `[title: Why the river turned dark]` | Title for this scene (on the first scene: the video's title card). |
| `[move]` / `[still]` | `[still]` | With Motion on: force this scene to become a moving clip / stay a painting (overrides Mix). |
| `[aiNote]` | `[aiNote]` | Small "Visuals are generated by AI" note in the corner. Added automatically on maps, globes and AI-painted flags; change the words with `aiNote="…"` on a map tag, or hide it with `aiNote=off`. |

**Tip:** you don't have to write these by hand. Press **Plan scenes (preview)** on the Script page: it shows every line as a task (what we see, where, camera, graphics, Motion), lets you edit the picture, place and time, and **Use this plan** writes them in as tags. The Script Writer does this for you automatically.

## Rules

- **A tag belongs to the scene it's written in.** A paragraph is a scene; a long paragraph may become two, and the tag goes with its own sentence. A line with only tags joins the scene above it.
- **Names are forgiving:** case, spaces and dashes don't matter, and display names work too — `[Big statistic reveal]` = `[bigStat]`.
- **Unknown names are skipped** and listed while the video is made ("Ignored unknown tags…"); they are never read aloud.
- **Numbers only come from you:** a graphic that needs a number takes it from the tag or from the scene's own words. If neither has it, the tag is skipped rather than inventing one.
- **Text you leave out is filled from the narration** — `[wordReveal]` reveals the scene's first words, `[markerHighlight]` highlights them.
- **Your tags always win** over the automatic choices for that scene.

## Tips for the best output

1. **Fix the pictures first.** One `[place: …]` per location and a `[scene: …]` on the lines that matter do more for quality than any effect.
2. **Tag the 2–4 key moments**, not every scene. One strong stat or map beats five weak ones.
3. **Write the facts in the sentence too** ("900 million internet users"), so the voice, captions and graphic agree.
4. **Give sequences their settings** for full control: `events="1947|Independence; 1991|Reforms"`, `steps="Plan; Build; Launch"`, `data="Delhi:21, Mumbai:24"`.
5. **One transition per cut** and one atmosphere per scene; atmosphere should suit the mood (rain, fireflies, embers), not decorate.
6. **Camera:** `pushIn` for emotion, `panLeft`/`panRight` to reveal, `pullOut` for endings, `zoomPunch` for a punchline.
7. **With Motion on**, use `[move]` on action scenes and `[still]` on quiet ones to steer Mix.
8. **Match the video type:** documentary → timelines, maps, quotes, then/now; tech → processes, charts, comparisons; fun → questions, myths, countdowns; story → characters and places, very few graphics.
9. **Check before you spend GPU time:** Plan scenes (preview) takes about 20 seconds and shows exactly what each tag will do.

## Camera moves

5 planned moves replace the scene's camera; the others are added on top.

| Tag | What it does | Length |
|---|---|---|
| `[zoomPunch]` | Quick punch-in on the video, then hold | 1.2s |
| `[slowZoom]` | Gentle Ken Burns push-in | 5s |
| `[shake]` | Camera shake for impact | 0.5s |
| `[pushIn]` | 2.5D dolly towards the scene (parallax by layer depth) *(replaces the planned camera)* | 5s |
| `[pullOut]` | 2.5D dolly away from the scene *(replaces the planned camera)* | 5s |
| `[panLeft]` | Camera slides left; near layers move more *(replaces the planned camera)* | 5s |
| `[panRight]` | Camera slides right; near layers move more *(replaces the planned camera)* | 5s |
| `[tiltUp]` | Camera rises to reveal the sky *(replaces the planned camera)* | 5s |

Settings: `intensity` (0.2–2) for the add-on effects, e.g. `[shake intensity=0.5]`.
